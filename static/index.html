How this experiment is computed · assumptions & sources

We solve iℏ ∂ψ/∂t = [−C ∂²/∂x² + V(x)]ψ, with C = ℏ²/(2mₑ) = 0.0380998212 eV nm² and ℏ = 0.6582119569 eV fs. The normalized initial state is a Gaussian with density standard deviation σₓ and positive central momentum ℏk₀; E₀ = Ck₀². Its mean kinetic energy is slightly higher than E₀.

Second-order central differences in space and the unitary Crank–Nicolson step in time (Δt = 0.008 fs) evolve the complex wavefunction in a background worker. Replays use stored numerical states. Δx is independent of the steady-state controls. Barrier widths and gaps are rounded to the nearest spatial cell; the actual geometry is shown above.

The calculation uses a large box, −80 to +80 nm, with zero-valued wavefunction at the outside ghost points. These walls reflect: they are not open boundaries. The experiment stops before appreciable probability enters the outermost 8 nm (threshold 10⁻⁷). The plotted window is a central crop; all probabilities integrate the entire box. Total norm conservation checks the discrete evolution, not convergence to the continuum: compare both spatial resolutions.

Left, structure and right are spatial probabilities. Only after the incident packet has passed and the outgoing packets have separated can left/right be interpreted as approximate reflected/transmitted fractions. A double barrier may retain probability in its well longer than the time window. We do not relabel a finite-time value as an asymptotic coefficient. Single-energy T(E₀) differs from the packet's spectrum-weighted transmission; the rectangle reference averages the continuum analytic T over the initial Gaussian momentum distribution.

References: Spencer & Ware, Computational Physics 430, Labs 8–9 (BYU); van Dijk & Toyama, Accurate numerical solutions of the time-dependent Schrödinger equation (2007). Numerical code was independently implemented; no external source code was copied.
